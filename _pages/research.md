My research is organized around two connected areas: **optimization algorithms** and **economics and computation (EconCS)**. Large-scale computation is a recurring concern across optimization problems, while EconCS motivates computational methods for market models and collective behavior.

## Optimization Algorithms

### Tuning-Free First-Order Algorithms

I designed a parameter-free method for nonsmooth convex optimization that does not require prior knowledge of the Lipschitz constant or the distance to an optimum. I established convergence guarantees for deterministic nonsmooth, stochastic nonsmooth, and stochastic smooth settings, and evaluated the method on L1 regression and SVM benchmarks. **Manuscript in preparation.**

### Adaptive Stochastic Approximation for Stochastic Programs

This ongoing project studies adaptive stochastic approximation methods for two-stage and multi-stage stochastic programs. My work includes implementing and evaluating the methods on stochastic programming benchmarks and comparing them with Dynamic Stochastic Approximation (DSA) and related baselines. **Ongoing research; manuscripts in preparation.**

## Economics and Computation

### [Rationalizing Collective Revealed Preferences with an Application in Fair Resource Allocation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23985)

With Chuwen Zhang, Zizhuo Wang, and Yinyu Ye, I work on learning collective revealed preferences through a computationally tractable surrogate market. My contributions include developing the Frank–Wolfe-based surrogate-consumer framework, deriving generalization bounds for several surrogate-consumer classes, and designing synthetic experiments. **arXiv preprint; submitted to ICLR 2027.**
